# E5 Findings and Limitations

E5 asked whether controlled typo augmentation makes the frozen E1 Combined TF-IDF + Logistic Regression detector more robust to misspelled prompts when the vectorizer, vocabulary, IDF and C = 1 are held fixed; only the D1 Train composition varied across six conditions. The primary augmented condition, `aug_random_p30_x1` (one extra copy per eligible text with typos in ceil(30%) of its eligible words), was chosen on D1 Validation alone: best mean Macro F1 over five typo-perturbed validation draws (0.9779 ± 0.0016 against 0.9744 for clean) with a higher clean validation Macro F1 (0.9843 against 0.9818); every sensitivity variant of the pre-registered rule picks it. The clean retrain reproduces E1's handoff scores to 1.3e-11, so the comparison is against E1 Combined itself.

The pre-registered primary comparison, Δ attack recall under one random typo on eligible D1 Test attacks at threshold 0.5, is +2.31 percentage points (95% paired class-stratified bootstrap interval +1.45 to +3.37, 1,000 resamples). This is not a data-size effect: the count-matched duplicate control does not beat clean (D1 Test Macro F1 0.9442 against 0.9451), and augmented minus duplicate under typos is +2.02 points (+1.16 to +2.99). The augmented model is also better on clean text: D1 Test Macro F1 0.9586 against 0.9451 (+1.34 points, +0.77 to +1.92) and D3 attack recall 79.6% against 70.4% (+9.3 points, +6.1 to +12.9); the D2 gain is not distinguishable from zero. Lexicon-targeted typos hurt both models more than random ones.

The cost is benign false alarms: NotInject false positives rise from 78/339 (23.0%) to 85/339 (25.1%), a paired Δ FPR of +2.06 points (+0.30 to +4.13). Mechanism: with frozen IDF the augmentation can only re-weight existing features. A typo removes the word from the 50,000-term word vocabulary 98% of the time, so the word-branch vector changes in 91% of D1 pairs while character 3–5-grams keep most of the signal; the augmented classifier shifts its contribution share slightly toward the character branch (66.5% against 65.0%).

Limitations: the threat model is synthetic single-character typos in words of at least four ASCII letters; split or merged words, leetspeak and homoglyphs are out of scope, and the human QA sample in `results/e5/qa/` estimates typo plausibility. D2 is small and partly German, so the targeted lexicon falls back to a random word for 60 of 116 texts. One training source, one annotation policy, one classifier seed; scores are uncalibrated; tF1 and t5 are provisional until E6 returns unified thresholds; intervals describe sampling variation of fixed test sets, not training-seed variation.
