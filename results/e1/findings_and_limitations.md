# E1 Findings and Limitations

E1 compared Word TF-IDF, character TF-IDF, and their concatenation while holding the classifier, training data, C grid, and evaluation code constant. All three representations selected C = 1 using D1 validation Macro F1 at the default 0.5 threshold. Combined TF-IDF achieved the highest validation score at 0.9818, followed by character TF-IDF at 0.9788 and word TF-IDF at 0.9772. After selecting source-only tF1 thresholds, the ranking changed on D1 Test: Word reached the highest Macro F1 at 0.9643, compared with 0.9595 for Combined and 0.9514 for Char. The differences are small enough that they should not be treated as conclusive without paired uncertainty estimates.

Transfer performance was substantially weaker. On D2, Combined produced the highest Macro F1 at 0.6992 with 46.7% attack recall and one false positive among 56 benign examples. Char reached 0.6674 Macro F1 with 41.7% recall and the same false-positive count. Word had higher recall at 56.7%, but its 19 false positives yielded a 33.9% FPR and reduced Macro F1 to 0.6118. On D3, Char had the best Macro F1 at 0.6799, but all three source-selected thresholds generated high false-positive rates: 48.7% for Char, 47.2% for Combined, and 53.6% for Word. This is evidence of source sensitivity rather than a reliable ranking for deployment.

These results use one deterministic training seed and one cleaned source split. D2 is small, so a single benign error changes FPR by about 1.79 percentage points. Dataset differences may reflect annotation policy, language, length, and template construction as well as model quality. The Logistic Regression scores are uncalibrated. Combined uses up to 100,000 features, twice the cap of either individual representation, so the comparison controls the classifier but does not equalize feature dimension. Exact overlap checks passed, while paraphrased near-duplicates and pretraining contamination remain unverified.
